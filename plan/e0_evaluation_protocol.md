# E0 evaluation protocol

Version 1 | 7 November 2026 | Bead `kenggo-n5n.2`

Evaluate move prediction, playing strength and complete-game behavior separately. Use this protocol with the [scope decision](e0_scope_decision.md) and [project plan](go_project_plan_v0.5.md). All numerical thresholds below are project choices, not measured results. E1 must settle the rules-dependent items listed at the end before model qualification. This document specifies evaluation; it reports no trained model or completed match experiment.

The [E1 game/data contract](e1_game_and_data_contract.md) fixes ordinary rules, caps, missing-data masks, stress populations, judgment coverage and absolute length/diversity/failure limits. Its [version-2 ending revision](e1_empirical_endgame_contract_v2.md) adds separately identified empirical KataGo scores and pass comparisons. Apply those limits alongside this protocol's gains, regression allowances, uncertainty and budgets; these contracts do not qualify a profile by themselves. Count accepted empirical scores as W/L, keep unresolved judgments adverse, and report referee version and cost separately from candidate play.

## Freeze each comparison before measuring it

Record the comparison ID, hypothesis, candidate and controls, primary metric, useful gain, regression limits, populations, versions, training seeds, evaluation seeds, scheduled looks and total cost cap. Include source/split manifests, input/result masks, ranks, clocks, rules/endgame adapter, checkpoint hashes, sampling or search settings and hardware. Link the code/configuration and W&B runs when the runner exists.

Use validation for selection and reserve final-test games, accounts, opponent configurations and random seeds for E7. Within validation, reserve a separate confirmation portion for each promoted comparison before tuning; publish its membership/counts but do not inspect its predictions or outcomes during selection. For matches this means fresh seed/color blocks, separate from floor-check and tuning blocks, against the frozen validation profiles. If there is too little data or budget for confirmation, record an exploratory result and defer promotion. Reusing an opened confirmation portion for a revised candidate is exploratory; a new confirmatory claim needs a fresh portion and a recorded budget.

Single-training-seed pilots select candidates for further work. A promotion comparison requires **three independent training seeds**, including all failed runs, and an independently budgeted confirmation run after candidate selection. Use paired initializations/data orders where the architectures permit; B's arms share each seed's saved random initial checkpoint. A's trained weights never enter B. Fix evaluation counts before running; do not add games until an interval passes. Negative and inconclusive results are valid outcomes.

## Move prediction and recorded-game outcomes

The primary imitation score is **rank-balanced negative log-likelihood (NLL), in natural-log units per move**. For each supported mover rank, average the loss over that player's eligible actions in each game, including recorded passes; average these game-side means equally; then average the rank means equally:

```text
loss(r,g,side) = mean over eligible moves t of -log p(recorded_action_t | pre_move_state_t)
NLL = mean over supported ranks r of mean over eligible (g,side) at rank r of loss(r,g,side)
```

This gives equal weight to ranks and game sides, not to moves or accounts. A long game does not outweigh a short game; a prolific account can contribute several game sides. Report account counts and the largest account weight. Also report pooled-move NLL and account-balanced NLL as diagnostics; do not switch the primary weighting after seeing results. A game side with no eligible actions has no mean and is an explicit exclusion.

Freeze one common eligible set for every arm in a comparison, using metadata and label availability before inspecting model outputs. Compare on identical positions and masks. A missing model output, illegal distribution or numerical failure is a failed evaluation, not a reason to remove a position. Use legal-action probabilities and stable log probabilities; a genuine zero probability on a recorded legal action gives infinite NLL. Any clipped diagnostic must be separately named. Use KataGo's raw `humanPolicy` for its imitation score; keep raw temperature-1 sampling, argmax, pass-corrected play and searched profiles separate.

For BASE use the population/rank-eligible set. For HIST, passes count as prior individual moves; short histories use the declared padding/mask rather than dropping openings. For PERSONA compare correct, default and same-rank shuffled identities on the same later games, with a matched additional-training population control; keep known and newly adapted accounts separate. For TIME use only duration-valid targets and the same metadata/missingness inputs in the full and metadata-only predictors. Keep E1's disjoint account cohorts, chronological whole-game splits, deduplication and training-only normalizers. Preserve full legality history regardless of model context.

For every set report candidate and retained games, game sides, accounts and moves; exclusion reasons; and coverage by exact mover/opponent rank, nominal clock tuple and missing-clock status. Report results by mover rank, move-number bin **1–60, 61–180, 181+** (individual moves), and main-time bin **10–<20 or 20–30 minutes** within the chosen byo-yomi family. These are reporting bins, not claims about semantic game phases. Publish joint-cell counts. Before seeing scores, mark any reported cell with fewer than **30 distinct mover accounts or 100 game sides** insufficient for a cell-level promotion claim. Do not quietly remove an unsupported rank from a frozen aggregate; narrow the advertised scope and start a newly recorded comparison.

Secondary measures use the same hierarchy and common eligible sets: top-1 move agreement; value mean squared error against the masked recorded result in the mover's [-1,1] view; and value calibration in ten fixed, equal-width prediction bins. Show mean prediction, mean result and counts in each bin. Unknown, timeout and forfeit outcomes remain masked from value evaluation. Value measures concern recorded human outcomes, not optimal play or human beliefs. TIME's primary error is mean absolute error in seconds; also report median/90th-percentile absolute error and overtime and long-think subsets, with the long-think cutoff frozen from training data.

### Uncertainty for recorded games and training runs

Compute paired candidate-minus-control losses before aggregation. Moves within a game and games sharing either player are dependent. Use a **dyadic cluster-robust sandwich variance** for the weighted means/contrasts: retain both source account IDs, cluster all observations from a game, and allow covariance between games sharing an account in either color or role. Treat disjoint account pairs as independent; disclose that assumption and inspect account concentration. Use normal-approximation 95% intervals only with at least 30 accounts and no account carrying over 10% of the aggregate weight. A negative/undefined variance or failure of these checks is inconclusive, not zero uncertainty. E2 must verify the weighted estimator with synthetic shared-account, swapped-color and repeated-game cases before relying on it. [Aronow, Samii and Assenova](https://arxiv.org/abs/1312.3398) provide the dyadic method; its use for these weighted Go scores must be validated.

For three-seed confirmation report each seed, the mean paired gain and a Student-t 95% interval across the three seed-level gains (2 degrees of freedom). Separately report the data/match uncertainty for the mean per-example losses or scores across those fixed checkpoints. Both intervals must pass primary-gain and continuous-metric regression assertions; neither alone is a joint interval for training and evaluation uncertainty. Undefined or zero-width seed intervals are inconclusive for these statistical assertions; use the explicit invariant/reliability cases below instead of fabricating seed variability. Three seeds give limited evidence, and an inconclusive interval does not justify a larger run without a new budget. Never count moves or matches as additional training seeds. This caution follows the plan's [evaluation reference](https://arxiv.org/abs/2108.13264).

Handle two cases separately. A deterministic invariant may satisfy its regression check with an exact zero change when the implementation contract proves it unchanged and checks confirm identical outputs on the common set. For example, changing only identity on one frozen network must not affect its value computation under E3. Record that evidence; repeated equal measurements alone are not proof of an invariant. For binary failure-rate regressions, qualify **each of the three frozen checkpoints** using the block-based rate bounds below and require all three to pass; do not fit a t interval to three zero rates. This establishes reliability of the evaluated checkpoints, not a failure-rate guarantee for unseen training seeds.

## Training and evaluation opponents

Each panel manifest pins executable/source revision, network/hash where applicable, resource settings, randomness, rules and endgame behavior. Adapter compatibility is an E1/E2 gate; the names below do not assert equivalence to KGS Japanese rules or a human rank. Choose resource settings from interface/cost probes, then freeze them before learned-model comparisons. If an engine is incompatible or unavailable, record the replacement family and repeat setup before freezing; do not substitute one after seeing transfer results.

| Panel | Members and weighting | Use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| B training | E5a-qualified frozen **raw A** profiles, under the single/pool/curriculum arms. Record each arm's opponent sampling and curriculum rules. | Rollouts only; wins here are not transfer evidence. |
| Independent validation | Three equally weighted groups: two simple weak policies (uniform legal placement and capture-first); **GNU Go**; **KataGo self-play**. Within each group, weight frozen profiles equally. Start engine setup with two resource levels per engine. | A qualification, B floor check, selection and validation transfer curves. KataGo Human SL is a separate imitation/playing reference, not another independent family in this aggregate. |
| Reserved final test | Three equally weighted groups: a held-out simple policy based on local liberties; **Pachi without a neural network**; **Fuego**. Weight profiles equally within each group. Hold configurations, random seeds and candidate results out of selection. | E7 transfer after deployment profiles and claims are frozen. Report each group; this fixed panel is not a sample of all possible Go opponents. |
| Human calibration | Verified KGS cohorts at the proposed label and adjacent ranks, separately recruited and reported. | Human-rank claims only; never mix human and engine scores into one rank estimate. |

The weak policies are explicitly unranked. E2 must specify tie-breaking, legal placement filtering, pass/resign and endgame behavior in their manifests and test complete games. Any heuristic belongs to that named profile; it must never silently modify raw A. All opponents use the shared legality/endgame contract. The official [GNU Go interface](https://www.gnu.org/software/gnugo/gnugo_19.html), [KataGo repository](https://github.com/lightvector/KataGo), [Pachi repository](https://github.com/pasky/pachi) and [Fuego project](https://fuego.sourceforge.net/) identify candidate implementations, not validated adapters.

Final-panel setup may exercise scripted fixtures and matches among reference policies for legality, reliability and cost. It must not expose scores of A/B candidates or initial B checkpoints to model selection. The final test may reveal a floor effect; report that limitation rather than adjusting the panel using final results. A new final panel would require a new sealed evaluation.

### B's validation floor check

Before E6's training comparison, evaluate every saved initial B checkpoint and both weak reference policies against the proposed validation panel, using **20 color pairs per tested policy/profile combination**. Report all scores, caps and unresolved games. For each initial B seed require at least one weak opponent against which its conservative score bounds lie wholly between **0.05 and 0.95**; require the weak references to produce at least two different panel point scores separated by **0.05**, with no unresolved ambiguity large enough to erase that separation. This is a setup sensitivity check, not a strength or significance claim.

If all tested policies score zero, or the above sensitivity/reliability conditions fail, first check legality, perspectives and scoring. Then revise the weak profiles/resources or narrow the planned comparison, record every setup run and repeat the check. Do this using initial checkpoints and reference policies only. Freeze the final validation manifest before training; never make the panel easier in response to a trained B's results. Score against a separately versioned random-move training aid, if later introduced, remains training evidence.

## Match protocol, scores and intervals

Start from an empty 19 x 19 board with the E1-frozen rules/komi. A block consists of **two games with swapped candidate colors**, the same opponent profile and a recorded seed pair. Alternate the order of candidates/controls and colors to reduce resource/order drift. Use the same scheduled blocks for comparisons; keep independent random streams for each agent. Record both wall-clock/resource limits and any actual game clocks. Untimed B uses A's trained missing-clock and unknown-opponent-rank paths; calibration humans use the matching timed conditions. Report these contexts separately.

For exploratory validation/final score estimates start planning at **100 color pairs per opponent profile per checkpoint**; the 20-pair floor check is separate. Derive confirmatory counts from the sample-size check below using the actual assertion count, profile weights and E1 thresholds, before fixing the budget. Predeclare cheaper exploratory checkpoints at 25%, 50% and 75% of the learner-transition budget, with 20 pairs per profile; the 100% exploratory evaluation uses the 100-pair planning count. Confirmation counts must fit the assigned budget. If the measured cost or required precision does not fit, report exploratory estimates or defer the comparison. Counts do not override the 24/48 GPU-hour caps. At every B checkpoint compare against that seed's initial checkpoint on the same frozen panel, and plot by learner transitions and total GPU-hours.

Keep every scheduled game in the accounting. Let N be scheduled games, W/L/D known wins/losses/permitted draws and U unresolved games, including unfinished or unfilled slots. Require N = W + L + D + U. Administrative caps are not draws. An invalid move or process failure follows the E1-declared forfeit policy and remains a behavior failure; a disputed end, incompatible scorer or infrastructure abort without a valid outcome remains U. Preserve the original classification and any later adjudication. Do not replay a loss or failure until a favorable result appears.

```text
resolved-only diagnostic score = (W + 0.5 D) / (N - U), if N > U
conservative all-game score bounds = [(W + 0.5 D) / N, (W + 0.5 D + U) / N]
gain bounds = [candidate_lower - control_upper, candidate_upper - control_lower]
```

Use all-game bounds for decisions, not the resolved-only diagnostic. Apply the frozen group/profile weights to endpoints. If U = 0 the score is the plan's (wins + 0.5 draws)/games. Report scores by color, profile and family, with counts, reasons, exact terminal scores where valid, move lengths, latency, network calls and SGFs. More configurations of one engine do not create more independent families.

For a fixed engine panel/checkpoint's score or score gain, use **10,000 paired bootstrap resamples of complete color-pair blocks within each profile**, retaining all outcomes and the same resampling indices for candidate/control. Report the 2.5th percentile of the lower score/gain endpoint and 97.5th percentile of the upper endpoint. These are approximate 95% intervals conditional on the fixed opponent panel, not uncertainty about unseen engine families. Binary reliability gates use the exact construction below instead of percentile bootstrap, including at nonzero counts. Deterministic reruns of an identical random-input block are duplicates for uncertainty, not new independent blocks. Fewer than 20 distinct blocks in a profile is exploratory.

When a score/gain endpoint has zero empirical variance, a bootstrap interval must not collapse to certainty: use a bounded Hoeffding interval for that endpoint instead, except for a verified deterministic invariant. For independent block contributions with normalized weights w_i and range width R, its two-sided half-width is `R * sqrt(log(2/alpha) * sum(w_i^2) / 2)`; R = 1 for scores and R = 2 for paired score differences. Use alpha = 0.05, clip to the possible range and include the unresolved endpoints. Verify this fallback in E2. For human matches, resample whole opponents (all their color pairs) instead of pairs; use equal opponent weights and retain missing scheduled games. For training-method claims also apply the separate three-seed rule above.

**Binary reliability bounds:** use one-sided exact Clopper–Pearson binomial bounds for every count, including zero, within each fixed engine-profile/color stratum. There is one binary game outcome for that color per independent block. The trials must be identically distributed within a stratum: fixed checkpoint, opponent, clock/resource context and independently drawn block seeds. Do not pool heterogeneous profiles into one binomial count, or treat the two colors in a block as independent. Split changed contexts into prespecified strata. For n trials and x failures, the upper bound U solves `P(Binomial(n,U) <= x) = a` (U = 1 when x = n); the lower bound L solves `P(Binomial(n,L) >= x) = a` (L = 0 when x = 0). Here a is the one-sided tail allowance for that marginal bound. [NIST's exact-binomial reference](https://itl.nist.gov/div898/software/dataplot/refman2/auxillar/exacbici.htm) describes this construction.

For a rate-regression assertion over P profile/context strata, divide its adjusted alpha among **4P marginal bounds**: candidate upper and control lower, each in both colors. Let q_j be the frozen profile/context weights, summing to 1. The adverse-change upper bound is `sum_j q_j * (U_candidate,j,Black + U_candidate,j,White - L_control,j,Black - L_control,j,White) / 2`. For an absolute candidate-rate gate use its 2P upper bounds and allocate alpha across those. The union bound protects the weighted contrast despite dependence between colors or arms. For candidate upper bounds count every missing/unjudgeable game as a failure; for control lower bounds count only verified failures. Retain the scheduled n and report missingness. Apply each gate to all three frozen checkpoints with its own allocated alpha. This binomial construction is for the fixed engine panel; do not apply it to four correlated games per human. Human calibration scores retain the opponent-cluster method above; human failure-rate summaries are descriptive under that study, while qualification uses the engine panel and E1 stress gates.

Before freezing sample counts, check both coverage and precision for these bounds, including rare nonzero failures. With zero events the marginal upper bound is `1 - a**(1/n)`. A 1% marginal upper bound needs **299 independent blocks per profile at a = 0.05**, **528 at 0.005**, or **757 at 0.0005**; each color has n trials, not 2n. Use `ceil(log(a)/log(1-epsilon))` for a sufficient zero-event count at allowance epsilon, with the actual marginal a after both assertion and stratum allocation. Evaluate the weighted gate itself when stratum counts differ. A tighter E1 absolute limit needs more. For example, with n = 528, x = 1 and a = 0.005 the exact upper bound is about **0.01399**, so it cannot establish a 1% rate; a percentile bootstrap can incorrectly pass that case. Derive the confirmatory budget from these requirements rather than treating the 100-pair exploratory count as a qualification sample.

## Behavior scorecard

Measure A and B in full games against the fixed panel, with A broken down by conditioning rank. Add a separately identified stress suite of weak/random-generated positions. Keep held-out human recordings, generated positions and played matches separate; do not pool their denominators. Apply the same seeded starting states and opponent blocks when comparing profiles.

| Measure | Definition and denominator |
| --- | --- |
| Legality and runtime failures | Illegal actions / attempted decisions; games with any illegal action, crash or state/replay mismatch / scheduled games. One reproducible legality/history/perspective defect blocks qualification regardless of the aggregate rate. |
| Premature passes | E1's adjudicator marks a pass premature when it meets a frozen avoidable-loss/unfinished-play definition. Report marked passes / all passes, unjudgeable passes / all passes, and games with any marked pass / scheduled games. For decisions also treat every unjudgeable pass as potentially premature; no passes means a per-pass rate is undefined, not zero. |
| Caps, disputes and unresolved games | Games hitting a cap / scheduled games; games entering a scoring dispute / scheduled games; U / scheduled games. These flags may overlap. Report reasons and adjudication coverage; a resolved dispute still counts as a dispute. |
| Game length | Individual moves including passes. Report median, 90th and 99th percentiles for true terminations and separately all observed lengths with capped/unresolved records marked as censored. Do not interpret a cap as a completed game of that length. |
| Within-rank move diversity | On a frozen held-out position set, sample 32 actions per position at temperature 1. Report unique legal actions and pairwise disagreement `1 - sum_a c_a(c_a-1)/(32*31)`, then use the imitation hierarchy. Also compare rank/phase-specific move-category frequencies with human records under an E1-frozen categorization. A single recorded action per position cannot establish human conditional entropy; do not equate these diagnostics with measured human diversity. |
| Compute | Total training/reference/evaluation/failed-run GPU-hours, CPU/wall time, peak memory, inference median/95th-percentile latency and network calls. Separate warmup/probes from timed steady-state work while retaining their cost. |

Use the corresponding game/account or match-block uncertainty units for descriptive rates and differences; clustered passes are not independent trials. Binary game-level qualification and regression gates use the exact stratum bounds above. For length and diversity bootstrap whole games/blocks or use the recorded-game account method as appropriate. E1 must specify and validate the premature-pass adjudicator, move categories, stress populations, absolute behavior thresholds and minimum judgment coverage before seeing candidate qualification results. All required absolute gates must pass; missing judgments or an unset threshold cannot be interpreted as success.

## Useful gains, allowed regressions and selection

Freeze the controls below before running. A gain is positive in the favorable direction. For promotion require the **lower confidence endpoint to meet the useful-gain threshold**, and the upper endpoint of each adverse difference to stay within its regression allowance. Use the conservative unresolved-game endpoints. A pilot may suggest an effect without passing this rule.

| Comparison | Primary gain required | Controls and limits |
| --- | --- | --- |
| BASE / HIST / PERSONA | NLL reduction **0.02 nats/move** | BASE: rank-conditioned versus same unconditioned model; KataGo is a reference, not a required win. HIST: versus the retained history control at comparable training cost. PERSONA: correct identity versus both default and same-rank shuffled identity, plus the matched population-training control. |
| TIME | MAE reduction at least **max(1 second, 10% of control MAE)** | Full model versus identical-input metadata-only predictor on the same valid targets. 90th-percentile absolute error may increase by at most 5% of the control value. A zero-error control cannot be beaten. |
| SEARCH | Independent validation score gain **0.05** | Fixed search versus raw play; adaptive versus each of fixed, metadata-only and shuffled allocation. First pass E4's outcome-value gate. Adaptive allocation must be within 5% of each allocation control's measured network calls and latency; otherwise report a cost/performance tradeoff, not an allocation benefit. |
| RL | Independent validation score gain **0.05** over that seed's initial checkpoint | Compare single/pool/curriculum at matched learner-transition budgets. For one arm to beat another, require a further 0.05 paired gain under the same rule; report GPU-hours separately. Training wins are not the primary metric. |
| Distinct A settings | Absolute panel-score difference **0.05**, with the ordering fixed before confirmation | Both settings must pass all qualification gates. Rank-conditioned names do not imply this order or a human-rank calibration. |

For changes to A, overall and supported rank/phase/time-bin NLL increases may not exceed **0.02 nats/move**, and value MSE may not increase by more than **0.02**. For imitation extensions, panel score may drop by at most **0.02** and raw-play 95th-percentile latency may rise by at most **25%**. SEARCH has its separately declared simulation budget and cost gate instead of that raw-play latency allowance. For both agents, cap/unresolved/premature-pass game rates may rise by at most **0.01 absolute**, in addition to E1's absolute limits; the E1 length/diversity limits also remain mandatory. Rules correctness allows no regression. Apply regression checks to the same advertised groups, not just a pooled mean. Unsupported groups must be removed from the proposed release scope before a new comparison rather than waived after failure.

Keep one primary comparison per confirmatory hypothesis. If several variants, groups or controls support one promotion claim, enumerate all statistical gain/regression assertions first, including each seed's reliability bounds, and use Bonferroni intervals with per-assertion alpha = 0.05 / assertion count. Record verified deterministic invariants separately. Apply that alpha to the chosen normal/t/bootstrap/bounded construction, or divide it among the exact-binomial marginal bounds as specified above. For bootstrap intervals use enough resamples to resolve the requested tails, at least 100 observations per tail. Report ordinary 95% descriptive intervals as well. Do not select a winner from many ordinary 95% intervals. Human calibration below uses per-label 95% intervals and makes no simultaneous coverage claim for all labels.

Among eligible validation candidates, select the best primary metric; if the difference is below the useful-gain threshold, prefer lower measured inference cost, then lower training cost, then the existing simpler profile. Freeze that choice before confirmation and final test. If any required comparison is inconclusive or a gate fails, retain the existing qualified baseline or record a defer/drop decision. Investigate ineffective rank conditioning before adding extensions; E2 baseline readiness does not require beating KataGo. Full-board B learning requires the RL improvement rule and complete-game gates; general superiority of A training over self-play or another family needs a separately budgeted matched-resource control.

## Human-rank calibration budget

No human access or funding is confirmed. Budget **1,000 games per proposed labeled profile**, after freezing the checkpoint, rank claim and matching rules/time conditions:

| Cohort | Distinct opponents | Games each | Total |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Claimed KGS rank | 150 | 4, two per candidate color | 600 |
| Adjacent weaker rank | 50 | 4, two per candidate color | 200 |
| Adjacent stronger rank | 50 | 4, two per candidate color | 200 |

The five proposed labels (15k, 10k, 5k, 1k, 1d) therefore require up to **5,000 games and 1,250 opponent/profile assignments**; a person may fill several assignments, so this is not a unique-person count. For 1k the stronger neighbor is 1d; for 1d the weaker neighbor is 1k. The outer checks need 16k and 2d humans even though those ranks are outside the initial training scope. B would need its own calibration budget for any rank claim. These are proposed recruitment and match counts.

For planning only, 100 independent games at exactly 50% score have a Wilson 95% interval of about **[40.4%, 59.6%]**, leaving almost no margin. Repeated opponents reduce information. With four games per person, the planning design effect is `1 + 3*rho`; assuming within-person correlation rho = 0.25 gives 600 / 1.75 = **343 effective games**, and an illustrative Wilson interval near **[44.7%, 55.3%]** at 50%. At rho = 1 it is only 150 effective games. These are sensitivity calculations, not the analysis of clustered results or a guarantee of passing. [NIST](https://itl.nist.gov/div898/handbook/prc/section2/prc241.htm) gives Wilson intervals; the [cluster-design reference](https://www.bmj.com/content/345/bmj.e5661) describes the planning inflation.

Analyze actual outcomes with opponent-cluster intervals and unresolved bounds from the match protocol. For a label, require the target cohort's entire 95% interval inside **[0.40, 0.60]**. Also require the weaker-cohort point score >= the target-cohort point score >= the stronger-cohort point score, and the paired/opponent-cluster 95% interval for weaker-minus-stronger score to lie above zero; use independent cohort resampling when the humans differ. If adjacent evidence is inconclusive, the label remains provisional. Record recruitment, rank verification, cohort representativeness and repeated participation; do not infer KGS rank from another server's label.

There is one fixed analysis after the scheduled games; do not repeatedly test the interval or relabel the same results until they pass. Failure or insufficient recruitment yields engine-relative strength only. A later attempt needs a new frozen claim, fresh games and a new budget. `kenggo-n5n.3` must cost recruitment, participant time, administration and inference separately from the SL/RL pilot caps before any calibration is scheduled.

No count in this protocol expands those caps automatically. Record changes and evidence in the owning beads; this document is the evaluation contract, not a second task tracker.
